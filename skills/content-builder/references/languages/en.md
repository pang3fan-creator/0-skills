# English

Examples and habits, not laws. Verify against the site's existing English pages before assuming a preference.

## Register

- Second person, direct address: "you can drag a block", not "blocks can be dragged".
- Contractions are normal in body copy ("doesn't", "you'll"); a site that avoids them everywhere is already formal — match it.
- American vs British spelling is a per-site decision, and it must be consistent across the whole site (check the existing pages; the project profile often records it).

## Phrases that read as machine-written

- "In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-evolving landscape of…"
- "It's important to note that", "It's worth mentioning that", "When it comes to…"
- "not only … but also", "…, not just …"
- "delve into", "unlock", "seamlessly", "robust", "leverage", "elevate", "empower", "game-changer"
- "In conclusion", "Overall, …" as a paragraph opener
- Em-dash every other sentence; every paragraph the same shape

Also watch for the SEO-flavored version: "Whether you're a busy professional or a student, …" — a sentence that includes everyone tells the reader nothing.

## Habits worth keeping

- Concrete verbs: drag, resize, print, export, rename. Not "utilize", "facilitate", "optimize for".
- Numbers with units and context ("90-minute blocks", "10 saved templates"), not "many", "several", "a variety of".
- Say what costs money plainly: "PDF export is a paid feature". Readers forgive limits; they do not forgive surprises.
- Prefer a short sentence to close a paragraph. It makes the rhythm human and tends to land the point.

## Spacing and mechanics

- One space after the period.
- `12-hour or 24-hour` time and `MM/DD/YYYY or DD/MM/YYYY` dates are site-wide decisions — never mix formats within a site.
- Oxford comma: match the existing pages.
- Headings: sentence case or title case, again matching the site. Do not mix within a page.
